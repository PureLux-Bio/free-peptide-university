How to use the map

When you meet a new name, your first question is simply: which family? That single answer tells you roughly what kind of research it belongs to and what conversations surround it. A new compound stops being intimidating the moment you can place it.

The boundaries are useful, not rigid — some compounds are studied across more than one family, and some research blends families deliberately. The map is a starting point, not a rulebook.

Try this: next time you see a peptide mentioned anywhere, pause and place it in one of the seven families above before reading another word.
